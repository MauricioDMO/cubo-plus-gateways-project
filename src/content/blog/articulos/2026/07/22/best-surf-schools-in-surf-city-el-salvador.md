# Best Surf Schools in Surf City, El Salvador

“Surf City” is the stretch of La Libertad coastline that runs from Punta Roca to Mizata — a string of black-sand beaches with a break for almost every level, minutes apart from each other by bus or car. El Tunco has the rockier, more advanced breaks and the busiest nightlife; El Zonte is quieter, with a friendlier point break and most of its life centered on one main street; San Blas is smaller still, a beach break most people only reach on a day trip from one of the other two.

That geography matters more than any single school’s reputation. Which one makes sense for you depends less on which name you’ve heard and more on where you’re staying, how many days you have, whether you’ve surfed before, and whether you want one lesson or a full week of coaching. Here’s what each of these five actually looks like on the ground.

---

## Puro Surf — El Zonte

Puro Surf sits right on El Zonte point, and it’s built around structure: ISA-certified coaches, daily check-ins before paddling out, and video review after each session so you can see what your coach is talking about.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/puro-surf.jpg?resize=749%2C500&ssl=1)

Image by [Puro Surf](https://www.purosurf.com/what-to-expect-from-coaching-at-puro-surf-academy)

The weekly surf camp format includes coaching, board selection with your coach, and afternoon add-ons like yoga, mobility work, or an ice bath. Coaches assign you a board for the week, and you’re free to take it out for extra solo sessions between lessons if the swell is working. It’s set up for people who want to actually change their surfing over several days, not just stand up once and call it a win.

**Good for:** intermediate surfers or committed beginners who want a full week of coaching.

---

## Los 3 Hermanos Surf — El Zonte

A local, family-run school on El Zonte beach — the name refers to the three brothers who run it. Lessons are taught in Spanish, English, and French.

What comes up most in feedback is that instructors stay close in the water instead of drifting off to catch their own waves — which matters more than it sounds like when you’re a beginner trying to time a wave and could use someone right next to you calling it. Lessons here tend to run at El Zonte’s main point break, though for beginners they’ll sometimes drive you over to Palmarcito or La Paz , a gentler, more forgiving wave a short trip down the coast.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/WhatsApp-Image-2026-07-21-at-10.58.45-AM-1.jpeg?resize=749%2C800&ssl=1)

Image by [los3hermanos.surf](https://www.instagram.com/los3hermanos.surf/)

Board, bodyboard, and skateboard rentals run alongside the lessons, and they’ll also set up kayak or stand-up paddle trips if you want a day off from surfing without leaving the water.

**Good for:** a single lesson or a couple of sessions, especially if you want an instructor who stays close and won’t leave you to figure out timing on your own.

---

## Surfing Your Fears — El Zonte

This one is coaching for people who already know how to surf but feel stuck. The approach treats a stalled plateau as partly technical and partly mental, and lessons work through both: reading your specific fear or hesitation alongside the actual mechanics of your pop-up or bottom turn.

From April to October, when south swells start lighting up the east side of the country, they’ll also run custom trips out to Las Flores and Punta Mango, about 3.5 hours from El Zonte, chasing right-hand point breaks that don’t show up on the standard El Zonte-El Tunco lesson circuit. That’s a different kind of session than a first-timer lesson — it assumes you’re already comfortable in the water and want to push past a specific block, whether that’s committing to a bigger wave, cleaning up a bottom turn, or just getting past the hesitation that keeps you stuck at the same level.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/WhatsApp-Image-2026-07-21-at-11.08.16-AM.jpeg?resize=749%2C739&ssl=1)

Image by [surfingyourfears](https://www.instagram.com/surfingyourfears/)

Sessions are built around conversation as much as water time — understanding what’s actually holding you back before adjusting the technical side. If you’ve had multiple lessons elsewhere and feel like you’ve hit a wall, this is coaching aimed specifically at that wall rather than another round of the basics.

**Good for:** surfers who’ve plateaued and want coaching aimed at a specific mental or technical sticking point.

---

## Wayo Surf School — El Tunco

Wayo is based in the center of El Tunco. It covers more ground than most: surf lessons, board and wetsuit rental, a surf shop, scooter and motorcycle rental, lofts to stay in, and day trips to Tamanique waterfalls, Santa Ana volcano, and the Ruta de las Flores.

Private lessons run higher than the average one-hour session elsewhere on this stretch — but they come with instructors who surf at a competitive level, and a private session is the format most people book rather than a group class.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/wayo.jpeg?resize=749%2C718&ssl=1)

Image by [wayosurf](https://www.instagram.com/wayosurf/)

Wayo works more like a one-stop shop for people staying independently in El Tunco: rent a scooter to get around, rent a board for the days you don’t want a lesson, and book the transfer to and from the airport through the same place.

**Good for:** anyone based in El Tunco who wants surfing, board rentals, transfers, and day trips in one place.

---

## Surf Strong — Playa San Blas

Located in Playa San Blas, about 7 minutes from El Tunco. San Blas has a sandy bottom and a beach break that’s genuinely easier on beginners than the rockier breaks in El Tunco itself — a lot of whitewater to practice in before you’re dealing with a real takeoff.

![](https://getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ss-2.avif)

Image by [Surf Strong](https://surfstrongelsalvador.com/)

It’s a straightforward setup: board rentals and lessons, run by a local surfer, no bundled packages, no add-on services, no hotel attached. Rentals run around $10 for a full day if you’d rather practice on your own than take a formal lesson.

**Good for:** a first surf lesson ever, or a low-key rental day.

## Good to know

- **Pricing runs a wide range.** A single group lesson typically falls between $25 and $45, private lessons at the higher end can run $60 or more, and board rentals alone are usually $10-15 a day. Multi-day academy packages like Puro Surf’s are priced separately and include lodging.
- **Cash and card both work, but small operators lean cash.** El Zonte was one of the first places in the country to accept bitcoin, so some smaller shops and schools can take it — worth asking directly rather than assuming.
- **Distances are short but not walkable in the heat.** El Tunco, El Zonte, and San Blas are all within about 15-20 minutes of each other by local bus or tuk-tuk — useful if the school you want is based in a different town than where you’re staying.
- **Bring reef-safe sunscreen and a rash guard.** Sessions often run 60-90 minutes in direct sun, and boards and wetsuits are provided by every school on this list, so there’s no need to bring your own gear unless you already have a board you’re attached to.
- **Ask about instructor-to-student ratio before booking a group lesson.** It varies a lot between schools, and it’s the single biggest factor in how much individual attention you’ll actually get in the water.
