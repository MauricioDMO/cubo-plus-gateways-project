# El Salvador: Everything You Need to Know Before Visiting

We get some version of the same message every week: “We’re thinking about El Salvador. What do we actually need to know?” So here it is, all in one place, from someone who actually lives here.

---

## Before You Fly

Most visitors (US, Canada, UK, EU, and plenty of others) don’t need a visa for stays under 90 days, just a passport stamp at the airport. Make sure your passport has at least six months of validity left. San Salvador’s international airport (SAL) is the main entry point, about 45 minutes to an hour from the La Libertad coast, and the newer Pacific terminal has made things noticeably smoother for beach-focused arrivals. Direct flights run from several US hubs: Houston, Miami, Los Angeles, Dallas, Atlanta; so depending on where you’re starting, you might not need a connection at all.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/62033bebe9ff713da07c0933.jpeg?resize=749%2C422&ssl=1)

Image by [RT](https://actualidad.rt.com/actualidad/419449-bukele-inaugurar-ampliacion-aeropuerto-internacional)

Once you’re here, the US dollar is the official currency, so there’s no exchanging money or doing conversion math. Cards are widely accepted in restaurants and hotels, but keep some cash on hand for tuk-tuks and local markets, and Spanish is the official language, though English is common in tourist areas along the coast. A little Spanish still goes a long way with locals.

---

## Is It Safe? When Should You Go?

This is what everyone actually wants to know, so plainly: El Salvador’s security situation has changed dramatically over the past few years, and that shift is well documented — it’s a big part of why so many more travelers are showing up now. Still, use the same common sense you’d use anywhere: mind your surroundings at night, stick to well-traveled areas. The coast has a relaxed, welcoming feel, full of cafés, coworkers, and other travelers.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/MG_0794.jpg?resize=749%2C500&ssl=1)

As for timing, there are two seasons here, not four: dry season (roughly November through April) brings consistent sun and busier crowds, while rainy season (May through October) usually means sunny mornings with afternoon showers, a greener landscape, and better surf swells for some. Temperatures stay warm year-round, typically in the 80s°F, so pack light regardless of when you come.

---

## Getting Around and Where to Stay

A rental car gives you the most freedom if you’re planning to explore beyond one town, though if you’re basing yourself in one spot on the coast (which I’d recommend for a first trip) tuk-tuks and short shuttle rides will cover most of what you need. For beach life and a slower pace, the La Libertad coast is the move: El Tunco has more energy and nightlife, El Zonte is quieter with a wellness and remote-work scene. San Salvador is worth a day trip for museums or city history, but most visitors treat it as a transit point rather than a base.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/DSC00073-HDR.jpg?resize=749%2C500&ssl=1)

## Practical Extras

Wifi is reliable in hotels, cafés, and the growing number of coworking spaces along the country, and local SIM cards are cheap and easy to grab at the airport. Tipping is 10% at sit-down restaurants — check the bill first, since it’s sometimes already included.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/WhatsApp-Image-2026-06-24-at-10.56.03-PM.jpeg?resize=749%2C915&ssl=1)

Image by [surfcafesv](https://www.instagram.com/surfcafesv/?hl=es)

For packing: reef-safe sunscreen, a light rain jacket if you’re visiting in rainy season, sandals plus one sturdy pair of shoes for hikes or volcanoes, small bills for tuk-tuks and markets, and a reusable water bottle.

## Day Trips and Tours

Getting out for the day is easy too — the volcanoes inland, like Santa Ana or Izalco, make for a solid change of scenery from the coast, and tours can usually be arranged with a local guide rather than booked cold online. ## Surf Year-Round

If surfing’s part of the draw, there’s no real off-season here — swells run year-round, they just shift in character with the seasons. El Tunco and El Zonte both have their own personality as breaks, and there are a handful of quieter spots further down the coast worth exploring if you’re past beginner and want less of a crowd.

![](https://i0.wp.com/getawayselsalvador.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/DSC00905.jpg?resize=749%2C500&ssl=1)

The best trips here aren’t the rushed ones. Give yourself time to sit at a café, watch a sunset from the same spot twice, and let a town grow on you before moving to the next one.
